Knowing your rights ahead of time helps you stay calm and make safe decisions. These rights apply to everyone in the United States, regardless of immigration status.

## You do not have to open the door

You are not required to open your door to immigration officers unless they have a **warrant signed by a judge**. Ask the officer to slip the warrant under the door or hold it up to a window so you can read it. A document titled "warrant of removal/deportation" from immigration enforcement is **not** the same as a judicial search warrant.

## You have the right to remain silent

You do not have to answer questions about where you were born, your immigration status, or how you entered the country. You can say: *"I wish to remain silent."*

## You have the right to speak to a lawyer

You can say: *"I want to speak to a lawyer."* You do not have to sign anything before talking to one. Signing a document you do not understand can affect your case.

## Make a family plan

- Memorize or keep a trusted attorney's phone number.
- Decide who will care for your children and who can access important documents.
- Keep copies of identity and immigration paperwork in a safe place.
