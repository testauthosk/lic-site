DACA: support for those who grew up here

Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals is complex and has changed over time. Liberty Immigration Council helps you understand where you stand and what options may be available to you.

What DACA is

A measure of stability for people brought here as children

Introduced in 2012, Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals (DACA) is a policy that has allowed certain people who came to the United States as children to request temporary protection from removal and, in many cases, work authorization. Protection under DACA is generally granted for a limited period and may be renewed.

DACA does not provide lawful permanent residence or a direct path to citizenship on its own. For many families it has, however, meant the ability to work, study, and build a life with greater security — while longer-term options are explored.

Who DACA has generally applied to

Eligibility is detailed and fact-specific, and the criteria below are general guidance only. Whether DACA applies to your situation — and what is possible right now — should be reviewed with a qualified professional.

01

Came to the United States before turning 16.

02

Have lived in the U.S. continuously over the qualifying period.

03

Were present in the U.S. on the dates set by the policy.

04

Are in school, have graduated, hold a GED, or are an honorably discharged veteran.

05

Have no disqualifying criminal record and meet the program’s other requirements.

DACA is subject to ongoing change

DACA has been affected by ongoing litigation and policy changes. What is available — including new and renewal requests — can change over time and depends on current court rulings.
